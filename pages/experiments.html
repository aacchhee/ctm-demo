Fourier coefficients: a ramp with a jump

Let \(f\) be the \(2\pi\)-periodic extension of

\[ f(x)=\begin{cases} 0,&-\pi\le x<0,\\ x,&0\le x<\pi. \end{cases} \]

Thus \(f(x+2\pi)=f(x)\); in particular, \(f(\pi)=f(-\pi)=0\). On each period the function is zero for half the interval, then rises linearly before jumping back to zero.

Use the real Fourier-series convention

\[ f(x)\sim\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigl(a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\bigr), \]

with

\[ a_0=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\,dx,\qquad a_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\cos(nx)\,dx,\qquad b_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\sin(nx)\,dx. \]

Work out the integrals before checking your answers. Enter exact expressions: use pi, n, sin(...), cos(...), and (-1)^n. In parts B and C, \(n\) is any positive integer. A formula must work for all such \(n\), not just the first few. The checkers compare symbolic identities; if an unusual equivalent form cannot be verified, rewrite it using these functions.

Part C — a shift and a discontinuity

Now let \(g(x)=f(x-\pi/3)\), a shift to the right by \(\pi/3\). Write its Fourier series in the same convention:

\[g(x)\sim\frac{\alpha_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigl(\alpha_n\cos(nx)+\beta_n\sin(nx)\bigr).\]

Find \(\alpha_n\) and \(\beta_n\) for every positive integer \(n\), as explicit expressions in \(n\) (do not use undefined symbols \(a_n\) or \(b_n\)). Then find the value to which this Fourier series converges at \(x=-2\pi/3\). Use the one-sided limits there; the assigned value of \(g\) at a single point does not determine the Fourier-series sum.

Substitute \(x-\pi/3\) into the original Fourier series and use the angle-subtraction identities. Translation preserves the mean. The function is piecewise smooth, so its Fourier series converges at each point to the average of the left-hand and right-hand limits.
